# Institutional Succession

{% include gaam-meta.html %}

> This is an informative candidate concept for evaluation. It does not modify GAAM v0.9.0 requirements, schemas, profiles, vocabularies, or conformance semantics.

Institutional succession addresses merger, acquisition, dissolution, insolvency, mandate loss, operator abandonment, emergency administration, and legal succession. It separates transfer of authority, capability, records, accountability, keys, identifiers, and service obligations.

A succession plan should identify triggers, successor designation, interim custody, relying-party recognition, continuity controls, historical evidence preservation, status and revocation services, and predecessor closure.

## Evaluation questions

- Can the current GAAM model represent the concept without ambiguous interpretation?
- Do multiple implementation patterns require the same semantics?
- Is a draft profile, experimental schema, vocabulary, or behavioural vector necessary?
- What privacy, security, affected-party, market, and continuity consequences arise?
- What evidence would justify normative promotion?

## Historical competence versus current responsibility

An institutional transition may change the effective competence to issue, record or revoke assertions while evidence custody and present remedial responsibility transfer on different dates. The [temporal governance model](temporal-governance.md) requires those roles to remain distinguishable when evaluating predecessor authority, successor competence and historical records. Present custody does not automatically confer historical issuing authority, and past competence does not establish today's accountability.
